# Here To Make Friends

**Here To Make Friends** is a podcast about friendship co-hosted by **Liz Feldman** and **Jessi Klein**, closing each episode with a "ravioli moment" — a time a friend showed up for the guest. [[people/robby-hoffman|Robby Hoffman]]'s episode, *"Robby Hoffman Says Get a Girl a Gift!"*, was published **September 15, 2026**.

Densest source in the wiki on Robby's **parents' religious trajectory** and on the **mechanics of being outed at 17** — and it opens with Robby not knowing one of her two hosts was a host.

## Key Takeaways

- **Girls need gifts.** The episode's title bit, argued against a birthday charity donation made in Liz's name: *"it's part of the non-profit industrial complex,"* bring back **cash in a card**. *"Be my uncle."* See [[bits/girls-need-gifts]].
- **Bring nothing.** Robby's hosting doctrine and her anti-potluck position — *"the potluck of it all, that's very lesbian and we started a bad thing."* Scales with means: when she was broke it was all-hands-on-deck; now it's *"bring nothing."* See [[bits/bring-nothing]].
- **Correction on her parents' background.** Robby pushes back on the premise that her parents were Reform: *"they weren't reformed… I would say they were conservative."* Her grandparents were post-Holocaust first-generation American and Canadian Jews — **not kosher**, high-holidays-only, and *"all about assimilation, succeeding, working hard."* Her parents grew up in that, then were **"radicalized"** into the **Lubavitch** sect. See the contradiction note on [[themes/orthodox-upbringing]].
- **Her mother is autistic.** Stated plainly, with the mother's own corroboration: *"if you're asking if I had any friends, the answer is no."* Robby's frame for how her mother got pulled in — a loner, a seeker, offered a table to sit at. *"If you are at the end of an escalator with a pamphlet, she will take it."* Still seeking; currently **Jews for Jesus**.
- **Her father, from stories only.** *"Very charismatic and very popular and cool,"* and he had a car. He had *"a larger calling"* — Robby's comparison: *"it's like joining ISIS but maybe less violent."* She does not speak to him.
- **Outed at 17, in full.** Someone from school saw the end of an embrace with her girlfriend in the bathroom of the student bar and told one person; it *"spread like wildfire."* She lost her friend group for a few months; they came back. Her mother wrote her **two rhyming poems** asking if she was gay, then told her father, who sent her to a psychiatrist — Robby went only on the condition he came too. The therapist's verdict: Robby didn't need therapy, but recommended the parents stay on. See [[career/outed-at-17]].
- **She never came out.** *"I realized when Gabby came out, my wife came out. I never came out. I was outed."* Asked when she would have been ready: *"I don't know if I would have come out really. I probably just would have got gay enough that you knew."*
- **The ring pop.** Thirteen days into dating [[people/gabby-windey|Gabby]], Robby bought a ring pop and asked her to be her girlfriend. *"Gabby is the gayest I've ever been. I've never moved in quick."* See [[bits/ring-pop]].
- **A bought house and a rented apartment.** *"You rent in the city and you buy out."* The country house came from years of saving; the NYC apartment is a rental she calls her dream apartment. See [[career/country-house]].
- **The ravioli moment: her sister in the snowstorm.** First night at the country house, the key failed and the car broke down. Her little sister Debs drove an hour-and-a-half route that took two hours through a whiteout in a Nissan Versa to bring the spare key, losing phone service in the mountains while Robby's landlord was telling him nobody should be driving up. *"I still consider it a miracle that God returned me and my sister."* They bought the most expensive thing in the grocery store to celebrate.

## Details

- **Type:** not physical — *"somebody who's extremely themselves."* Until Gabby she hadn't realized humor mattered to her: *"Gabby is extremely funny all the time."* The deal-breaker isn't an unfunny partner, it's a partner who needs to be found funny — a five-year relationship is cited where *"it was a problem for her that she wasn't funny, not for me."*
- **"I have a right to know."** Gabby said "I love you" once, then stopped; Robby confronted it in legal register. She concedes on air: *"I don't have a right to know. It's not really a right."*
- **The $400 bartender.** Instead of a [[projects/wake-up|Wake Up]] premiere she threw a watch party at her apartment with a private bartender, an idea she'd banked years earlier from a classy friend's party and assumed would cost $10,000. It cost $400 for the night.
- **Mother's housing, declined.** Her mother lives with Robby's brother (a single father) and six-year-old niece in Canada, one bathroom between them, squabbling with the niece over mirror time. Robby offered a down payment on a three-bedroom, two-bath if they'd carry the mortgage; her mother rejected the house over its bus route — she needs to board early enough to get a seat and read. See [[themes/family]].
- **Family as a mutual-aid network:** *"a lot of my family is successful. It's not just me… we all step up when we can."*
- **Crispy towels.** The poverty bit gets revisited — the one towel that exfoliates, and not knowing towels weren't inherited. See [[bits/crispy-towels]].
- **The phone voice.** Sales work gave her a corporate code-switch she can no longer summon on command — *"it's like if I was suddenly speaking to Spectrum on the phone."* She used to police her voice toward *"a regular girl"*; now *"however I'm being or talking is fine."*
- **Annoying, by her own account and everyone else's:** *"I've been told I'm annoying my whole life. I am annoying."* Stand-up is the release valve — *"almost an exaggeration of me… me on like 100%."*
- **High school:** she dated a male model. *"Guys liked me."*
- **Vinyl.** A limited pressing of [[projects/wake-up|Wake Up]] is coming; she hand-signed five and *"screwed up a couple of them."*
