# RIP Jordan Jensen

**RIP Jordan Jensen** is the solo podcast of comedian [[people/jordan-jensen|Jordan Jensen]]. The "RIP" is a joke: Jordan is alive and touring. [[people/robby-hoffman|Robby]] has been on **twice**, which makes it her second-most-returned-to show after [[projects/anniewood|Anniewood]].

The two women first met on air as opponents, in a heated gender-semantics argument on *Bein' Ian with Jordan* (Oct 2024; see [[people/jordan-jensen]]). By Ep. 75 they describe each other as friends who trade business advice.

## Key Takeaways

- **Two appearances, seven months apart.** Ep. 60 (Mar 2026) is the reconciliation, mid Wake Up press surge. Ep. 75 (Oct 2026) is two friends ribbing each other at the end of a long day.
- **Ep. 75's spine is Jordan's life, with Robby as the interventionist.** Robby wants her out of a mouse-infested Brooklyn studio and into a doorman building on the Upper West Side: *"It's time to have a home."*
- **The episodes are dense on Robby's childhood**, especially her mother and food, weight and attention. See [[themes/family]].
- **Robby's death-and-nothingness argument** shows up here nearly word for word as on [[projects/you-made-it-weird|You Made It Weird]] the same month. See [[themes/death-and-afterlife]].

## The two appearances

### Ep. 60 (March 2026)

Covered in full on [[people/jordan-jensen|Jordan's page]]. In short: the two address the "(perceived) beef" from Bein' Ian and agree it was a productive disagreement. They talk OCD, success, confidence and self-worth, and how Robby stays safe on tour. Robby gives her [[projects/wake-up|Wake Up]] self-rating (*"punch for punch one of the greatest stand-up comedy specials in recent history"*) and the earliest version of the annual-checkup story: her brother Menachem was weighed in his underwear and flagged as underweight. Her father is *"somewhere in Nevada."*

### Ep. 75 (October 2026)

**Released October 19, 2026.** Both arrive exhausted, and the episode opens with the texts in which one of them tried to cancel.

**Top surgery and binders.** Jordan asks how Robby feels about it now: *"Best thing I've ever done."* Robby wore a binder for two or three years beforehand and recommends one over a sports bra (no elastic digging in), sized properly from the size chart. She'd only consider transitioning if you could *"literally"* become a man: *"I do love being a woman."* See [[career/top-surgery]].

**LA versus New York.** *"One's a dump, one's a shithole. You got to toggle."* [[people/gabby-windey|Gabby]] loves New York. Since New York no longer allows short-term rentals, the couple would sooner buy an apartment there than keep renting. The last time Robby lived in New York, during [[projects/the-chris-gethard-show|The Chris Gethard Show]], she was too poor for it to be a good life: a rental in Williamsburg under the elevated train on Broadway.

**Narcissism.** Jordan's theory is that comedians are narcissists and self-loathing at once. Robby pushes back. She'll admit to *tendencies*, but *"narcissism is way overused."* She claims *"zero"* entitlement (she's never late) and too much empathy: she can't watch an old man waiting at a bus stop in the cold. She also takes aim at hyperlabeling in the queer community, and repeats her mother's line: *"Not everybody has to like you."*

**Family across two worlds.** She's the most media-trained person in her family and *"even I'm a disaster."* She talks to six of her nine siblings; some *"don't agree with my life."* Her little sister works for her now (see [[people/yehudis-hoffman]]). Bringing family backstage used to scare her. Then [[people/schmuly-hoffman|Schmuly]] met [[people/pete-davidson|Pete Davidson]]. Her question now: *"Is it better for Pete Davidson to think I'm cool or to give my brother Schmuly the time of his life?"*

**The mother, food and weight.** See [[themes/family]]:
- Her mother was heavy when the kids were small and later lost about 80 pounds through Weight Watchers and swimming. Robby remembers shop staff ignoring her before the weight loss.
- Being poor made her mother afraid the kids looked underfed: whole milk, full-fat cheese, nobody went to school hungry. The checkup story from Ep. 60 is retold.
- At the rich school she attended on subsidy, a friend's mother had pinned magazine ads of Halle Berry inside the pantry. Robby used to eat that friend's lunch and throw out her own, and only realized as an adult that the friend had been anorexic.

**Comfortable in problems.** Robby says she was *"only comfortable in problems"* for a long time and is only now learning to accept that there are fewer of them. People raised comfortable go skydiving because they need a thrill; *"I find life thrilling enough."* Her version of a self-worth origin story: *"I was kind of feral, left to my own. All I had was me"* — she liked herself, and everything around her was the problem. Then the [[bits/born-jewish-and-poor|born-Jewish-and-poor]] riff.

**Death.** Her grandfather (Zadie) told her before he died that she was *"living her life now."* She and Jordan agree their lives come first and their careers second. Robby's afterlife argument, and her recommendation of Netflix's *Surviving Death*, are on [[themes/death-and-afterlife]].

**Plugs.**
- **A comedy record.** Secretly Canadian pressed **500** after telling her there had been demand (*"I thought it was coming from my mother"*). She got five and is giving one to her mother, a pack-rat *"OG library lady"* who has always mailed her comedy records. See [[projects/wake-up]].
- **The Beacon Theatre**, November 12: *"I have to sell more."*
- **Tour:** the UK, Europe, Australia and New Zealand (February).
- **Late-night TV:** Jimmy Kimmel had her on once for the special and asked her back for the Brooklyn shows, even though she had nothing new to promote.

Also in passing: she's going camping with *"a bunch of lesbians"*, and Gabby is bringing her own car in case she needs to leave early. She'd played the Comedy Cellar's downstairs room and bar and the Village Underground that week.
