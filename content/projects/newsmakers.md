# Newsmakers (NPR)

**Newsmakers** is NPR's interview series, framed as bringing "the biggest names in politics, business, sports, arts, and culture out of the headlines and into the interview chair." [[people/robby-hoffman|Robby Hoffman]]'s episode, hosted by **Juana Summers**, was published **September 3, 2026**.

It is the most direct interrogation of the criticism against Robby in the wiki — Summers asks about the they/them material as a *danger* question rather than an offense question — and it contains the fullest telling of the [[career/wga-strike-speech|2023 WGA meeting]] recorded so far.

## Key Takeaways

- **"If I can't talk about it, who can?"** Robby's core defense of her non-binary material, staked on her own position: *"somebody who's clearly in the queer space, who cut my tits off, who could be they/them at the drop of a hat."* See [[bits/no-backsies-they-them]] and [[themes/queerness]].
- **She concedes the frame rather than the point.** On whether the jokes are dangerous: *"I have a different definition of dangerous. I consider dangerous like violence. Um, but I think yeah, they could be dangerous and maybe there is a more philosophical interpretation of dangerous. So maybe I'm open to that."*
- **Symmetry, not immunity:** *"I think it's fair if they're mad about it. I also think it's fair for many of us to laugh at it… You're allowed to be you, I'm allowed to be me."* Her invitation to critics is to go do 15–20 years and get on the stage themselves.
- **No responsibility for interpretation.** Asked if she feels responsible for how audiences read her jokes — *"No, I don't."* But she grants the reaction its legitimacy, because she has it too: *"sometimes something hits me personally… and I do take it personal and that's fine too."*
- **A joke she retired — the only one on record.** Her little sister worked a Starbucks open until at least midnight and either walked home or took the last subway, through what Robby calls *"a really sketchy few blocks."* To get through them the sister would **imitate a physical disability**, or perform mental illness by carrying an umbrella rain or shine. Robby found it funny and heartbreaking at once — *"they were heartbreaking at the time"* — reworked it into a bit, and later dropped it: *"I wouldn't do that now. At the time I didn't know anything of PC. I didn't know where my line about hurting people or not was. Still figuring it out."* Her verdict holds both halves: *"it's offensive and it also sucks to be poor."* No method behind the call — *"I can't tell you that there's a science to it, but I felt a way to it."* Notable as the counterweight to her [[bits/no-backsies-they-them|they/them]] position on the same episode: there she claims the subject as her own and doesn't budge; here the target was someone else entirely and she stopped without needing a rule.
- **"You need to sit down."** The fullest [[career/wga-strike-speech|WGA]] account: 1.5% dues, printed financial statements, questions about a board of six or seven drawing million-dollar salaries and about the guild's real-estate holdings — then a public scolding and a room that booed. Her retrospective line: *"a rich cis white straight man is telling a young queer girl to sit down when she's got a question."*
- **Nothing is off-limits, but plenty is untouched.** Her formulation for topics she doesn't do: *"I don't talk about beach balls. I don't have anything on beach balls."*
- **Free speech cuts both ways.** On peers who say they can't say anything anymore: *"bro, you just did, but other people had a reaction to it."* On Jimmy Kimmel: sympathetic, but *"he hasn't been free probably since he started his show"* — he works for a corporation.
- **She wants a network late-night show.** *"I would love to. I'd be excellent at it. Call me."* The caveats are practical: she can't read a teleprompter (vision, and *"I just go off"*), so *"record me and leave room for edits… if you're going to hire me, it's at your own risk."*
- **"We've monetized it."** Her framing of a lifetime of getting in trouble for talking: *"That was the same me in third grade… And you know what, baby? We've monetized it."* See [[bits/lil-rob]].

## Details

- **First time on stage.** A comedy loft in Montreal — *"some dude's apartment"* — hosted by **Morgan O'Shea**. She lasted a minute before the cigarette smoke, a pre-show shot and nerves nearly made her faint, sat down, went outside for air, came back fine and asked to finish her remaining four minutes. Denied. She came back the next week. See [[career/first-open-mic]].
- **The mother as silent example.** Her mother's 12-year once-a-week university degree is retold here with a sibling's summation: *"she's the definition of putting one foot in front of the other."* Robby's gloss on her mother's style — not emotionally available, openly disclaiming expertise (*"I don't know nothing… you're going to have to fight your own battles and look out for each other"*), but present *"rain or shine."* See [[themes/family]].
- **Never an overnight success:** *"I've always been the slow and steady, just keep at it."*
- **Money, stated plainly.** *"I love money. I'm very driven by money."* She names the mechanism without apology — glorification of material wealth was taught, and *"the marketing worked on me"* — then adds the poor-kid's addendum: *"when you're lacking, you also need it first before you can even want it."* Now: *"it's as good as I thought it would be and better."* Still nervous to lose it, still braced for it going away *"which has happened to me many many times."* See [[themes/money]].
- **Claimed by everyone.** Jewish, LGBTQ, women, New Yorkers, Canadians — asked if she owes any of them representation: *"No, I just feel like I have to be me."* Her stated assignment is one person: *"I was given one little person and it's my responsibility to figure out this person."*
- **Braces as a class tell.** She'd like to fix her teeth and won't, because the timing would announce itself: *"you know when somebody came into some money where they had the braces."*
- **The landlord interrupts.** Mid-interview Robby takes a call from her landlord about a blocked line — the clog was a hairball, though *"there was a tampon in the line"* — and clarifies for the record that she is not the one flushing tampons. Incidentally confirms she **rents**. See [[career/country-house]].
- **Closing ask to her own audience:** welcome newcomers rather than gatekeep. *"Don't say, 'Oh, I was here first. This is mine.' Show them around."*
