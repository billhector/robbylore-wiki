# The Political Scene (The New Yorker)

**The Political Scene** is *The New Yorker*'s politics podcast. [[people/robby-hoffman|Robby Hoffman]]'s episode — *"Everything Funny Hurts a Little"* — was a **live taping at the Cascade PBS Ideas Festival** in Seattle, moderated by New Yorker senior editor **Tyler Foggatt** and published **July 7, 2026**.

It is the most explicitly political long-form interview in the wiki: roughly half the runtime is Robby's class analysis rather than comedy-career material. See [[themes/politics]].

## Key Takeaways

- **"Classism is the conversation."** The episode's spine. Robby's argument: red-versus-blue was talked about for 200 years before it became the status quo, so poverty deserves the same 200 years — *"I want this to be the only conversation."* See [[themes/money]].
- **The repetition is deliberate.** She names her own interview habit as strategy: *"I'm repetitive. Every interview I go back to this."* When a backlash flares she redirects — *"that's fine. Back to here. People still poor."*
- **Both parties, one system.** *"Nominal differences at the top… think about who was on Epstein's island. They were hanging out, and they weren't hanging with us."* She names the government-lobby-corporation loop and the DNC's handling of Bernie and Biden. Politicians she credits: **Mamdani** (*"tremendous"*) and **Bernie Sanders** (*"I would take a senile Bernie"*).
- **Trump is funny and that's the problem.** *"Extremely. One of the funniest ever, unfortunately so."* But the funny doesn't buy immunity — *"'cause he's also really dumb. So he toggles those two."*
- **The backlash inventory.** Of everything in [[projects/wake-up|Wake Up]], the only group that came after her was **the pit bull community**; the abortion-age-of-10 joke drew nothing from pro-lifers. The **celiacs** came next, after a gluten question on [[projects/call-her-daddy|Call Her Daddy]]. Her read: *"the celiacs are allergic to gluten and comedy apparently."*
- **"Nobody is spared"** as a stated ethic — she jokes about her own communities too, to make the point that siloing into victim narratives serves the people in power. *"It's the people versus the power."*
- **Everyone finds the same things funny**; the difference is permission. Evidence offered: she tours nearly every state, and audiences who arrived not wanting to like her ask for a photo anyway.
- **Being offended is survivable.** *"I don't think being offended is that bad. I think being poor is bad."*
- **[[projects/hacks|Hacks]] arc credit: none.** Asked how much she shaped Randi's storyline — *"Nothing."* They wrote the part for her. Her acting self-assessment: *"I'm not necessarily the Meryl Streep version of acting. I'm more the Adam Sandler,"* with [[projects/dying-for-sex|Dying for Sex]] as her one real "push."
- **[[projects/unentitled|Unentitled]] stays sealed.** Asked for any update on the HBO series, she gives a flat *"I can't… Nothing."*
- **The other Robby Hoffman.** A Florida man of the same name has run for president every four years; lawn signs reading "Robbie Hoffman for president" appeared in Robby's own neighborhood and fans sent photos. Her first read was scriptural — *"is this like Moses? I've been told that I have to run."*

## Details

- **Where the funny comes from:** *"I come from a funny people and everybody was funny."* It only registered as unusual when she was *"put against normal people."* Named funny family members: her mother and her brother Schmuly. See [[themes/family]].
- **Stand-up as concentrate:** the recurring formulation appears here as a THC metaphor — stand-up is *"so concentrated,"* life is *"a little more like a hybrid."* She also disclaims authorship of her own material: *"I don't choose my thoughts… I don't want to think of half the things I think about."*
- **Growing up boyish:** self-monitoring as a teenager — sitting "more girly" to keep the prospect of a boyfriend alive. *"Now I don't even think about it."* See [[themes/queerness]].
- **No private room, so no exit:** she credits her tolerance for disagreement to having had nowhere to storm off to. *"If my sister and I were fighting… we gotta go to sleep somehow. We had to figure it out."*
- **The homophobic-brother exchange:** her brother, a security guard assigned to work a Pride event, objects to *"the whole gay agenda."* Robby: *"Well, I'm a gay person."* Him: *"Well, not you."* Her read: *"he doesn't even connect… I think people sometimes just say stuff because the heart isn't there."*
- **On populist credentials:** Foggatt raises her own joke that she's the only person who can actually prove she worked at McDonald's — *"it's true because of the pictures."* See [[career/mcdonalds-job]].
- **Grandfather cameo:** the Biden-is-too-old argument routes through [[people/nathan-letovsky|her grandfather]] — *"my grandfather's gotta lay down, he's gotta take a lay down. I wouldn't have my grandfather going to Europe and then flying here."*
