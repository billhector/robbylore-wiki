# We Got Company

**We Got Company** is [[people/sebastian-maniscalco|Sebastian Maniscalco]]'s podcast, a comedian-to-comedian conversation about the working life of stand-up. [[people/robby-hoffman|Robby Hoffman]]'s episode was published **September 24, 2026**.

The most craft-focused interview in the wiki — and the one where Robby is visibly nervous, saying so repeatedly and unprompted: *"this is the most nervous I've ever been for any podcast and I barely get nervous… I'm pretending that I'm just talking, but really I'm not."*

## Key Takeaways

- **Where "Robby" came from.** She was born with her Jewish name and took **Uncle Rob's** — a Vermont uncle, *"nobody ever said anything bad about my Uncle Rob"* — as cover when she started comedy, because she was still at the accounting firm and got caught anyway by a write-up in a Montreal paper: *"Is this you?"* See [[people/uncle-rob]] and the contradiction note on [[people/robby-hoffman]].
- **Voice first, material second.** She was told finding your voice takes ten years and had the reverse experience — *"Starting stand-up was my voice. So then the material came after."* Her image for it: whatever the subject, it *"went through me… like an Instagram filter"* and came out fresh.
- **The pandemic van.** With clubs, airports, hotels and Airbnbs closed, she bought a **$1,500 van**, put a bed in it — *"classic rape van. I didn't want it to look like anybody was in it"* — and drove the South playing anywhere open: Georgia, Florida, Little Rock. See [[career/pandemic-van-tour]].
- **Mulaney begged her.** Her correction to the assumed power dynamic on [[projects/wake-up|Wake Up]]: *"John Mulaney begged me to direct the special… People are going to say, how did I get Mulaney? But really the story is Mulaney, how did he get me?"* Her analogy is "Hurt" — everyone credits Johnny Cash, but it was a Nine Inch Nails song. See [[people/john-mulaney]].
- **She had never done an hour.** [[projects/i-m-nervous|I'm Nervous]] was a Crave commission in Toronto for **CAD $25,000** (*"about $6 here"*) with **six weeks** to prepare, built from 15-minute chunks — the first full hour she ever performed was the one being taped. Her manager suggested waiting for a better offer; she declined: *"I don't wait for anything. A dollar today is worth more than a dollar tomorrow."*
- **She will not clip.** No TikTok account, no tripod at the back of the room, no crowd-work clips — *"it's just not in me to do it,"* even knowing it cost her speed. The outcome: *"I'm huge on TikTok. I don't have TikTok. People throw me up there."* One consequence she likes: *"the only place you could see my stand-up is to come see me."*
- **Confidence as the only asset she had.** *"I literally only had me… It's not ideal. It's not great… but it's not nothing. It's close."* The five-people-in-the-room corollary: *"they're the ones who came. Why are we mad at them?"*
- **Simultaneously nervous and confident.** *"My baseline is nervous… but I'm very comfortable being nervous."* Strangers are easy, intimates are not — she grew up unwatched (a single mother of ten didn't make recitals or parent-teacher nights), so *"10,000 people I don't know, I take that any day over you and my sister just there."*
- **Media with no medium wasted.** Stand-up, scripts, acting and animation are slots — *"It's like Plinko. I have a slot for all the things I can do and I don't have to lose them."* Stand-up the night before a writers' room makes her *"a lot fresher… I come in kind of from the gym."*

## Details

- **Odd Squad was film school.** A six-month writer's-assistant contract became over three years and an Emmy. *"We did 80 episodes a season. I learned to write good and fast and get them out, not think too much."* See [[projects/odd-squad]].
- **The accounting exit.** She made **CAD $32,500** a year, loved being paid every two weeks, and did stand-up at night — until the firm's face-time culture made the double life untenable. She had also been accepted to **fine arts school** for portraiture and turned it down: *"there's no way I'm making any money. I'm in no mood to be poor."* See [[career/accounting-to-standup]].
- **Her sister is her assistant.** Little sister **Yehudis** now works for her and is *"literally living her life as Randi, who I play on [[projects/hacks|Hacks]]."* Robby's standing offer to siblings in sales: use my name — *"why can't I nepo my family if I can?"* See [[people/yehudis-hoffman]].
- **Her mother's note on the material:** *"you never tell them I tried, you never tell them I did the best I could."* Robby: *"Ma, that's not the joke."* Aside for the record — *"that's the best she could. I wouldn't tell anyone that. It wasn't great."*
- **Date in your class.** Dating a rich girl didn't take: *"the stinginess of a rich person's house. You can't go in the fridge."* Against her own upbringing, where *"my mother said take, take, take."* See [[themes/money]].
- **The watch, again.** A year of research before spending **$3,600**, worn daily for seven years — *"it's getting cheaper and cheaper by the day."* See [[bits/cost-per-wear]].
- **The luxury is the absence of an alarm.** *"Every day I don't wake up with the alarm and have to take the subway to work is a miracle… it's not gotten old."* She won't queue for ice cream, and gets *"depressed for them"* watching people who do.
- **She doesn't travel and doesn't want to.** Rich classmates backpacked Europe on summers off while she worked; her version of finding yourself was staying home. *"It's a thankless job to take care of yourself"* — the line she gave a struggling younger sister at 19: *"you have a 19-year-old kid that you have to keep fed, educated, fulfilled."* She moved out at 17, as most of the siblings did.
- **On the road:** hotel all day, Judge Judy and QVC. She took the subway to her own sold-out Town Hall show and surprised a ticketholder — *"where do you think I'm going?"* With Gabby she'll splurge: first class, same movie, press play together.
- **Autism, raised by her:** *"maybe this is the autism people always want to diagnose me with."* See [[bits/ai-is-autistic]].
- **Glasses since fourth grade** — minus 3.5 and minus 3, prescription sunglasses, green eyes. She quit contacts partly by stretching solution to make it last.
- **Toronto sensitivities** were the early version of the current backlash: *"why are you making fun of bisexuals?"* Her answer then and now — *"even me as a dyke somewhere on the gender spectrum… why are we this protective class?"* See [[bits/no-backsies-they-them]].
- **The jacket story.** She approached Maniscalco at the Comedy Store only because she was wearing a Top Man suede jacket and he said *"nice jacket"* first; she'd already retired the practice of telling comedians she's a fan. Her family had made shirts to see him — sister [[people/kaya-hoffman|Kaya]], her French husband Bruno, first stand-up he ever saw.
- **Zero personality disorder** — the bit from her first special about weekend hobbyists — resurfaces as her explanation for doing nothing. *"What do I do on the weekend? Nothing. 'Cause I got a personality."* See [[bits/zero-personality-disorder]].
- **Boiled eggs on the megabus** gets its fullest telling here. See [[bits/eggs-on-the-bus]].
- **Self-tapes** *"should be illegal"* — she begs for Zoom auditions rather than do her own media production.
- **Christmas:** her second-ever Christmas present from Gabby was a pair of Prada shoes she'd wanted since childhood. *"I'm loving Christmas. I've done three now."*
- **Touring pace:** two shows a week, deliberately. *"I'm quality over quantity. I'm not running myself ragged here. I want to be here till, God willing, 120 minimum."*
