# You Made It Weird

**You Made It Weird** is comedian Pete Holmes' long-running interview podcast. [[people/robby-hoffman|Robby]] appeared in an episode released **September 30, 2026**, during the [[projects/wake-up|Wake Up]] tour. Holmes and Robby both perform regularly at the Comedy Store, which is where he saw the set that prompted the booking.

The episode is unusual for how much of it Robby spends on **process** — how she writes, why she gets nervous, what she needed to start — rather than on the press-tour beats.

## Key Takeaways

- **Holmes' thesis is "freedom"; Robby's correction is "fear."** He opens by praising how unflinching she is on stage. Robby's reply: *"I live in fear. I live a life of fear… just because I'm afraid doesn't mean I don't do it."* She compares stand-up to her treadmill — six months of daily use and never a runner's high, but she gets on anyway.
- **"Way less embarrassing than working for someone else."** The line Holmes says he's been quoting since he first heard her say it at the Comedy Store. Robby elaborates: she still sees people with lanyards going back into office buildings after lunch and doesn't miss the feeling. See [[themes/money]].
- **Nobody watched her as a kid, so being watched now is the hard part.** Strangers are easy; a person of importance in the room is not. When the owner of the Comedy Cellar said he was excited to watch her, she told him she'd rather he didn't: *"I grew up without a father and nobody watched me."* Expands the account on [[projects/we-got-company]]. See [[themes/family]].
- **The six months were a Fringe deadline.** A theater-major friend, Steph, told Robby she was too funny to be an accountant and booked her a Fringe show for that summer: 45 minutes of stand-up, the rest filled with interviews. Robby did a brand-new five minutes every week at the same Monday mic until she had 45. See [[career/accounting-to-standup]].
- **The first set, retold.** The loft, the shot, the indoor smoke, the near-faint a minute in, the four minutes she was refused — consistent with the NPR version. See [[career/first-open-mic]].
- **"It takes 10 years to find your voice."** Older comics in Montreal's clubs told her this; what people noticed first in her was the voice. Anything she talked about came out *"through a Robby Hoffman filter."*
- **Reluctantly gay, for financial reasons.** At 17 she felt she couldn't afford it. See [[bits/cant-afford-to-be-gay]].
- **Meeting Gabby, retold.** Both waiting for Ubers outside a venue; *"I'll wear a mask"*; the [[bits/dora-the-explorer|Dora the Explorer]] line; and the first real question, *"Do you come from money?"* See [[people/gabby-windey]].
- **On [[people/john-mulaney|Mulaney]]:** the classiest man she's met, who turned out to be able to *"throw it down at dinner."* She expects to stay nervous around him for another two or three years.
- **Meaning of life:** *"the point of life is to live a life."* See [[themes/death-and-afterlife]].

## Details

### On craft

- **Material arrives mostly formed.** She writes ideas down as they come — *"I kind of work like Eminem"* — and the first time she does a bit on stage is close to how it stays. Same with scripts: the first draft is usually the draft.
- **She tried out a new premise on air**: [[bits/megans-pick-a-spelling|Megans need to pick a spelling]].
- **She doesn't watch stand-up.** She watches documentaries instead (*"I'm a doc head"*) and recommended *The Barkley Marathons* to Holmes. Her case against watching your peers became [[bits/let-the-straights-march|let the straights march]].
- **Stolen material doesn't worry her much.** When she left Toronto someone took her joke about the price of Duo-Tang folders. She dropped it: *"I have a million more where that's coming from."*
- **Hecklers mostly don't happen** because she doesn't come off as someone who wants to be interacted with. When it does happen, it's [[bits/office-hours|office hours]].
- **Comics she contrasts herself with:** [[people/pete-davidson|Pete Davidson]], whose loose stage presence she admires as her opposite, against her own *"Taylor Swift camp"* — works hard, prolific, tries hard.

### On the episode's other threads

- **University on scholarship at 17**, living alone, working 25–30 hours a week in telemarketing and sales. A student-accounts officer questioned the \$60 she'd set aside "for whatever comes up" out of a roughly \$300 stipend.
- **KPMG for the free laptop.** She chose the firm's paid internship because it came with one, which she says changed her life. See the laptop note on [[career/accounting-to-standup]].
- **The mother at parent-teacher night**, summoned after two years of no-shows and asking only whether Robby was failing. See [[themes/family]].
- **Born in Brooklyn, opinions to match:** *"Not only do I have an opinion on everything, I have a big one about it."*
- **The tour is full.** She's not adding more dates — *"I am one person"* — and is now sleeping enough to give every show her best.
