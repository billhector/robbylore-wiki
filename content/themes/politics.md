# Politics

[[people/robby-hoffman|Robby]]'s politics collapse into one axis, and she says so every time she's asked: **not red versus blue, but the people versus the power.** *"I've never been red or blue. I am for the people, by the people, of the people to the day I die."*

Because she reduces everything to class, this page overlaps heavily with [[themes/money]] — that page holds the personal-finance and poverty material; this one holds the institutional argument.

## Key Takeaways

- **"Classism is the conversation."** Not *a* conversation — the only one. *"The haves versus the haves not is the conversation… Until we can get a better grip on that, I can't see myself really screaming about anything else."*
- **The 200-year argument.** Her answer to "we're having the conversation now, what's the problem?": red-versus-blue was talked about for two hundred years before it became the default frame, so poverty gets the same. *"200 years on poverty. And then maybe I'll be okay."*
- **Repetition as tactic, admitted.** *"I'm repetitive. Every interview I go back to this."* When a backlash flares — a loaf of bread, a dog — she redirects: *"that's fine. Back to here. People still poor."*
- **The two parties are one system.** *"Nominal differences at the top. Think about who was on Epstein's island. They were hanging out, and they weren't hanging with us."* Across Democratic and Republican administrations in her childhood: *"we never got a drip."*
- **Her mechanism for it:** *"Companies through lobbies pay the government. The government collects money from people and distributes it to the other companies."* If that's the system, *"they work for each other to keep each other in power."*
- **Democrats ushered Trump in.** Her position on his second win, from Los Angeles: *"so many people around me were stunned when Trump won again. And I was like, are you joking?"* She faults the DNC for running Biden (*"putting some senile guy up there"*) and then Kamala Harris late — *"they knew that they were putting in someone that… it was crazy to usher that in."*
- **Politicians she credits:** **Mamdani** — *"I think Mamdani is tremendous"* — and **Bernie Sanders**, whom she'd take at any age. *"I would take a senile Bernie… Even if he has half a thought, it's better than most of the full thoughts."* On the squashing of Bernie before the DNC, and Occupy Wall Street, as conversations deliberately closed.
- **Trump is genuinely funny and it isn't a defense.** *"Extremely. One of the funniest ever, unfortunately so."* She credits the McDonald's stunt and the straws as real bits, then blocks the follow-up — can a funny person get away with anything? *"No, 'cause he's also really dumb. So he toggles those two."* Her read is that he's losing his own people: *"I don't think people are forgiving him."*
- **Populism has a proof requirement.** Her own joke, cited back to her: she's the only person who can actually prove she worked at McDonald's — *"it's true because of the pictures."* See [[career/mcdonalds-job]].
- **Institutional suspicion generalizes.** *"Many of the systems that we have faith in were created by systems that did not deserve our faith."* And: *"the more I learn about the world, the more I learn that so many things that should be, aren't."*
- **The WGA is the worked example.** Her questions about guild salaries and real-estate holdings, and the public scolding that followed, are her own template for how an institution protects itself. *"It's hard to be right early."* See [[career/wga-strike-speech]].
- **She has floated running, mostly as a bit.** Post-Netflix: *"maybe I throw my name in the hat… if you like Trump or Bernie, you kind of end up at me."*

## Why it matters

Robby is not a political comedian in the usual sense — she has no topical act and very few Trump jokes. What she has is a **single frame she refuses to leave**, which is why the political interviews and the poverty interviews are the same interview. The clearest statement of the method is her response to being asked about offense: *"I don't think being offended is that bad. I think being poor is bad. See, I go right back to here."*

It also explains the shape of her audience. She tours nearly every state and treats that as evidence against the premise that political tribes find different things funny — *"I think it's an allowance thing."* People arrive not wanting to like her and ask for a photo anyway.
