# Money

Money — specifically class translation — is the through-line of [[people/robby-hoffman|Robby]]'s comedy. She grew up in a Montreal apartment with **twelve people sharing one bathroom**, attended **a rich Jewish private school on subsidy**, left **a $32,500-a-year KPMG accounting job** for stand-up, and now talks openly about how she calibrates wealth in her marriage and her audience.

## Key Takeaways

- **Twelve people, one bathroom.** Roach and lice infestations. Cellophane on windows for heat retention. Brothers wearing hand-me-downs that Robby still references by name (*"my brother Manakim's pants"*).
- **Rich-Jewish-school-on-subsidy:** Robby got into a private Jewish Montreal school via interview; the principal offered to take just her, but grandfather insisted brothers come too. The brothers were out within a year.
- **KPMG salary:** $32,500/year as an accountant. She gave herself **six months** to break into comedy from there. *"Had I stayed there, I would have made partner. I was determined not to be poor."* See [[career/accounting-to-standup]].
- **First payday:** an HBO Canada option for $1,000 — she celebrated by buying one beer.
- **First big sale:** $65,000 for a network show six years before the 2026 FT profile — *"For me it was, like, a year's salary."* She spent $3,600 of it on a 1991 Rolex Air-King and still runs the [[bits/cost-per-wear|cost-per-wear math]] daily.
- **"Loud" money vs whispered wealth:** *"When you're poor and you grow up, it's the opposite of people who grow up wealthy, who enjoy a discreet wealth when they're older. They like cashmere, but no logo, you know. They're whispering. I'm still screaming."*
- **Mother's generosity lesson:** as a kid, Robby watched her mother give $5 to a homeless man on a late-night subway when the family was "tight." Mother: *"Sometimes it's important to give, to remind yourself you're in a position to give."*
- **Dating-up:** [[bits/pull-rich-men|"How to pull rich men"]] is a literal Robby tutorial — she frames her Lubavitch + class background as the training set.
- **"I'm the last American dream."** — her closing line on [[projects/talk-easy|Talk Easy]] sits on top of this whole theme.
- **Tipping rant:** says tipping is a residue of racism and employers should just pay people.
- **"The calling is a nightmare"** (South Beach Sessions, May 2026): pursuing a calling while poor has *"no guarantee of payoff"* — *"you're extending your poverty for a very long time."* Accounting was the rational hedge: *"I could have gotten paid every 2 weeks. That to me is heaven."* The money fear is health-grade — *"I consider financial health and health."*
- **Money trauma is collective.** *"Capitalism and money systems are extremely traumatizing… built on the backs of many… and it collapses often."* Her survival instinct thrives in the storm — she **sold her first show during COVID** and worked through the writers' strike.
- **First-class "apartheid"** (South Beach Sessions): flying alone she sits in coach (*"they hate you in the back"*); the splurge she allows herself is **first class with [[people/gabby-windey|Gabby]]**. *"The fact that we even on a plane call things class… it's so dehumanizing"* — but *"having money is way better than I thought."*
- **Rich-weird vs poor-weird** (NYT The Interview, Jun 2026): money didn't make Robby's world less weird — *"I just see rich people in their weirdness now."* Her verdict: *"the rich weird is way weirder and worse… there's a humanity that's missing."* The tell is generosity — in a poor house everyone's in the fridge (*"take, take — you want a Coke?"*); rich houses have the biggest fridge but *"nobody can go in the fridge."*
- **The $7.99 raspberries** (NYT The Interview): poverty's residue is emotional, not arithmetic. *"I don't care if the money's in the account. I'm not in a place emotionally to drop $7.99 on a little thing of raspberries."* Marrying [[people/gabby-windey|Gabby]] — who also grew up meager — means *"we speak the same language."* Robby frames their shared-class background as *"literally dating inside our faith."*
- **"Comfort is a rich concept"** (NYT The Interview): the episode's spine. *"I've never heard the word growing up. Comfortable. We're not comfortable — there's roaches in the sink, the snow's coming through the window."* The rich are the ones uncomfortable; they don't talk about money or politics, while Robby's mother *"was screaming about money on that phone from morning till night"* in a one-phone, thin-walled apartment.
- **"Classism affects everybody"** (NYT The Interview): Robby's stated us-vs-them is *"always the rich versus the poor"* — not red/blue, which she calls a distraction from *"bigger shit going on."* *"It shouldn't be a new conversation. It's the conversation."*
- **[[people/uncle-eddie|Uncle Eddie]] and $1 gas** (NYT The Interview): when Montreal gas hit a dollar, her great-uncle *"didn't leave the house"* — *"Are you kidding? Gas is a dollar."* Robby cites it as the day-in-day-out poverty logic that still shapes how she buys (take a little off the top for one thing she loves forever; be careful with the rest).

## Why it matters

Robby's stand-up persona is *materially* class-aware in a way many comedians' isn't — she names dollar amounts, names jobs, names the precise mechanism of upward mobility. Her family is still in Montreal; she still flies coach alone; she still SuperCuts on Tuesdays.
