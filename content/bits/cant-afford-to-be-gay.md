# Can't Afford to Be Gay

[[people/robby-hoffman|Robby]]'s account of realizing she was gay at 17, told as a budgeting problem. Pete Holmes, on [[projects/you-made-it-weird|You Made It Weird]] (Sep 2026), named it *"the reluctant homosexual."*

## Key Takeaways

- **The timing.** She was 17, living alone, taking four or five university classes and working nearly full-time in telemarketing and sales. *"I just could not handle another thing happening to me."*
- **The plan had been to marry rich.** Boyfriends from wealthier circles were an option, and she thought she could do well at it. *"So when I found out I was gay, I'm like, I can't do it."*
- **The math.** Applying the traditional template she grew up with, a couple needed a breadwinner, and *"as soon as I was gay, I thought I might be the boy one."* She had watched her five brothers pay $100 for a movie date. *"That's a bridge too far. I can't afford it."*
- **The punchline:** *"If you can afford women, okay. But until then, you're getting dick… Dick is free and affordable."*
- **It sits inside a bigger "why me?"** She compares herself to Moses, who didn't want the job either (*"he's like, I have a lisp"*): first she had to be gay, then she discovered stand-up. *"You want me to be gay? You want me to be an artist? Artist is for rich kids."*

## Why it works

It treats sexuality like any other cost, which is how Robby treats everything (see [[themes/money]]). The stereotype she's working with — the butch one pays — is one she lived and admits was wrong (*"I learned that there's a lot, you know, not everything has to be the same"*). The reluctance is real; the joke is the accounting.
