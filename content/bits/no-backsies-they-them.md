# "No backsies on they/them"

Recurring [[people/robby-hoffman|Robby]] bit: once you've taken **they/them** pronouns, there's "no backsies." Frames her own non-trans queerness, [[career/top-surgery|top surgery]], and the politics of pronoun fluidity from the inside.

This is the material that has drawn her most sustained criticism — more than the pit bulls, the celiacs or the abortion-age joke. It's also the criticism she engages with most directly rather than deflecting.

## Key Takeaways

- Robby has [[career/top-surgery|had top surgery]] but **does not identify as trans** — she frames it as gender dysphoria, not transition.
- The "no backsies" framing positions pronoun adoption as a one-way door, with comic finality.
- Recurring across podcast appearances; in stand-up rotation, including [[projects/wake-up|Wake Up]].
- **Her standing defense is standing, not argument** — she claims the right from her own position rather than from a theory of comedy. *"If I can't talk about it, who can?"*

## The defense

Pressed on NPR (Sep 2026) with the strongest version of the objection — that critics don't merely find the jokes offensive but **dangerous** — Robby splits the difference rather than dismissing it:

> *"Well, I have a different definition of dangerous. I consider dangerous like violence. But I think yeah, they could be dangerous and maybe there is a more philosophical interpretation of dangerous. So maybe I'm open to that."*

Then the claim of standing, in her own terms: *"as somebody who's clearly in that space, in the queer space, who cut my tits off, who could be they/them at the drop of a hat… I like to cope and explore through jokes. And I feel like I should be more able to do that than say some of my counterparts."* The counterparts get named — *"shout out to Joe Rogan and people who talk about it and have no experience"* — with the explicit caveat *"it's no dig."*

Her settlement is symmetry rather than immunity:

> *"I think it's fair if they're mad about it. I also think it's fair for many of us who laugh at it… You're allowed to be you, I'm allowed to be me. You could say what you want, I could say what I want."*

And the invitation, which doubles as a gatekeeping move: *"go work 15, 20 years, keep at it, get on to this stage and say whatever the hell you want to say."*

## The Toronto precedent

The same argument has an earlier, smaller version. Coming up in Toronto, where she says there were *"more sensitivities"* for stand-up, the pushback was about a different group: *"why are you making fun of bisexuals?"* Her answer then is the answer now, with the self-implication foregrounded:

> *"Even me as a dyke somewhere on the gender spectrum — I don't know what's going on, something's off obviously — but we can't be made fun of? Why are we this protective class of, oh, I'm so delicate? Some of us are delicate, some aren't, just like some men are delicate, some aren't."*

She admits she grew to enjoy the fight: *"I started liking the argument a little bit. Not to be edge-lordy about it."*

## Contrast

Set against **the one bit she has actually retired** — the [[projects/newsmakers|Starbucks walk-home joke]] about her little sister, dropped on the same episode — the boundary becomes legible. There, the target was outside her experience and she stopped without a rule: *"I can't tell you that there's a science to it, but I felt a way to it."* Here, she claims the subject as her own and doesn't budge.
