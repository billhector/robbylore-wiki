# Lil Rob

A recurring rhetorical move rather than a stage joke: [[people/robby-hoffman|Robby]] speaks **directly to her childhood self**, usually to report progress. She uses **"Lil Rob"** and **"little Riv"** — the latter from **Rivkah**, her given name. See [[people/uncle-rob]] for where "Robby" came from.

## Key Takeaways

- **The address:** *"Hey, Lil Rob. I see you. You got to trust me."*
- **The report is always about speed:** *"I am getting us there as fast as humanly possible."*
- **And about difficulty the child can't be expected to grasp:** *"You don't know what I'm dealing with out here. You don't know the systems and the rich and the nepos and the everything. It is crazy. You would never understand. You're too young to understand right now."*
- **The child is specified concretely** — *"the little kid in me who's got roaches and is really impatient and wants to get out of here and how are we going to figure out money."*
- **It governs her risk-taking.** On [[projects/we-got-company|We Got Company]] the same address is what overrides the nerves: *"I look at little Riv, me as a kid, and I go, Rob, I am trying to get you there as soon as possible… So I'm always rooting for myself."*
- **The mission statement version:** *"I was given one little person and it's my responsibility to figure out this person and to get this person to be the best version that they could be."*

## Why it works

It reframes ambition as an **obligation to a dependent** rather than a want, which is the same structure she uses for self-care generally — *"it's a thankless job to take care of yourself… you have a 19-year-old kid that you have to keep fed, educated, fulfilled."*

It's also what lets her talk about money without apology. If the money is owed to a specific poor child with roaches, wanting it isn't greed. Runs directly into [[themes/money]].

The counterpart move is the one she makes about her own trouble-making: *"That was the same me in third grade… And you know what, baby? We've monetized it."*
