# Girls Need Gifts

[[people/robby-hoffman|Robby]]'s intervention on birthday etiquette, delivered on [[projects/here-to-make-friends|Here To Make Friends]] and giving that episode its title.

## Key Takeaways

- **The trigger:** the birthday email announcing *"a donation has been made in your name."*
- **Robby's ruling:** *"It's part of the non-profit industrial complex and it needs to be called out."* The corporate version is worse — a large gift parceled out as individual tributes, with the write-off intact.
- **What to do instead:** *"we need to bring back cash in a card."*
- *"Be my uncle. Thank you for being my uncle."* — the check from an uncle or a nana is the format she's after. *"I'll take a $25 check."*
- **On "no gifts" invitations:** *"Bro, buy a gift."*
- **The rule, stated:** *"Girls need gifts."* Partners and close friends are obligated; general party guests are not.
- **Corollary she also argues, apparently contradictory:** for a party you bring **nothing**. The two coexist because the obligation is the *person's birthday*, not the hosting. See [[bits/bring-nothing]].

## Why it works

It's an etiquette hot take that's really a class argument in costume — the donation-in-your-name is a gift form available to people who already have everything, and Robby's objection is that it launders a transaction into a virtue. Cash in a card is the unembarrassed version. Same engine as [[themes/money]]: the performance of not-needing is itself a wealth signal, and she declines to perform it. Her own test on the day: *"am I rich enough to say no gifts? And I decided I'm not."*
