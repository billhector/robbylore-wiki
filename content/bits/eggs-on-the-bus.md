# Boiled Eggs on the Bus

[[people/robby-hoffman|Robby]] took the megabus to early gigs and ate **boiled eggs** on it. Both halves of the bit are true and she commits to both.

## Key Takeaways

- **It started as logistics.** The bus stops at a 7-Eleven or a shelter. *"I'm not going to grab a bag of Doritos. I need something of sustenance. I'm about to do comedy."*
- *"I'll take four eggies. What can I do?"* — and, flatly: *"I don't even love the eggs."*
- **The second function is territorial.** *"Nobody sits next to you on the bus. And that's a good thing."*
- **Timed deliberately:** *"When they're filing on is when I open my first egg. I'm going to town on the egg."*
- **No remorse about the seat:** *"I'll drop a little yellow on that seat. I don't care."*

## Why it works

It's a poverty bit that refuses to be pitiable. The eggs start as the only real food available at a bus-stop and end as a weapon — the same move she makes across [[themes/money]], where constraint gets re-narrated as competence rather than deprivation. It pairs with the towels bit ([[bits/crispy-towels]]) and with the megabus-era touring described in [[career/pandemic-van-tour]].
