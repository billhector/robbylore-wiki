# The Pandemic Van Tour

When COVID closed the clubs, [[people/robby-hoffman|Robby]] bought a **$1,500 van**, put a bed in the back, and drove to whatever parts of the country were still open for live comedy. It is the clearest single illustration of the slow-and-steady working method she describes everywhere else.

## Key Takeaways

- **Stand-up closed first.** Her last pre-shutdown show was at the Improv on **March 13**; when rooms reopened the booker had changed and she was back to square one. *"The guy loved me. I'm telling you, this is insane. I'm writing an email — can you tell them that you loved me?"*
- **The van cost $1,500.** Bed in the back, deliberately anonymous from outside — her own description: *"classic rape van. I didn't want it to look like anybody was in it."*
- **Why a van at all:** *"airports were closed, and hotels were closed, motel, no Airbnb, nothing."*
- **Where she played:** the South, mostly. **Georgia, Florida, Little Rock, Arkansas** — *"I just went to anywhere that would have me."*
- **Sometimes accompanied.** Her girlfriend at the time joined for some of it — *"the van isn't, you know…"*
- **The grim arithmetic of who was allowed out.** Her recollection of the risk guidance six months in: *"if you weren't fat or old, you were fine. I remember thinking, that's me. I'm actually the privileged person in this case."*

## Why it matters

The van period is the bridge between the Canadian club years and the American touring career, and it is where the *"I'll go anywhere that'll have me. I'll talk to anyone"* posture — which she now cites as the reason her audience has no demographic — was actually built. It also feeds the recurring claim in [[projects/the-political-scene]] that she is *"boots on the ground… I've been to almost every state,"* which she uses to argue that political taste in comedy is about permission rather than difference.
