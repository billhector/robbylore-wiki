# First Open Mic

[[people/robby-hoffman|Robby]]'s first stand-up set lasted about **one minute**, and she was refused the other four. She went back the next week.

## Key Takeaways

- **She went looking for the worst room on purpose.** Her question at a Montreal club: *"Where's the best place to bomb?"* — she may not even have known the word yet.
- **The venue was a "comedy loft"** that turned out to be *"some dude's apartment… a very hard loft. I don't know if it was legally livable, if there were facilities."*
- **She was 20-something and sheltered**, out with friends, in a room she guessed was full of thousand-year-olds who were actually 30 to 35. *"I was kind of like a studious kid."*
- **The collapse was medical, not comedic.** A pre-show shot plus indoor cigarette smoke plus nerves — a minute in she went dizzy, nauseous, and started greying out, the way she used to faint as a teenager. She sat down rather than fall.
- **Fresh air fixed it.** She went outside into the Canadian winter, came back *"good as new,"* and asked the host, **Morgan O'Shea**, for her remaining four minutes.
- **Refused.** *"No, it's not how it works. You just come back next week."*
- **She came back.** *"And it was great from that second week on."*

## Why it matters

Robby's own framing is that the first set didn't count as a failure at all, and she reaches for a boxing analogy to explain why: *"it's kind of like a boxer when the doctor comes in and they take him out of the fight. They check the eyes. It's not like he's not a great boxer, but there was a medical."*

It's the earliest instance of the disposition she describes throughout [[projects/we-got-company]] — *"slow and steady, just keep at it"* — and it predates the accounting-firm double life described in [[career/accounting-to-standup]].
